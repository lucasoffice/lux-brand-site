01Project of Record

Space #D-232 holds retail for six months while the flagship is renovated.

5,644 SF temporary pop-up at The Mall at Short Hills, priced to the HBC Issue for Permit, LL Review, Bid Set dated 07/01/2026 and supplemented by the Puur+ Complete Set 12-06. Union labor, August 17 start, October 16 opening, April 1, 2027 tear-down.

Suit Supply occupies Space #D-232 at The Mall at Short Hills, Short Hills, New Jersey for approximately six months while the flagship is under renovation. The authoritative tenant area is 5,644 SF, confirmed by Suit Supply for permitting after three different figures appeared across the issued documents. LUX Brand Company carries every hard construction trade on the job excluding millwork supply — demolition, framing, carpentry, GC-built items, millwork installation, mirrors and glazing, paint and wallcovering, flooring, electrical, fire protection, signage installation, FF&E logistics, and nine weeks of general conditions.

The number is lump sum at $554,499.96 with 13.14% overhead and no contingency carried. Where a document conflict remained open at bid, the line was carried as a marked allowance and the conflict was written into the RFI register rather than absorbed into the price. The July 22 cost-impact register ranked fifteen questions by dollar exposure so the items that actually move the total were closed by the awarding authority before construction rather than discovered in the field in September. Scope was leveled trade by trade against the HBC set, and quantities were rebuilt against the confirmed 5,644 SF figure.

02Scope of Work

Thirteen trades in, twelve carve-outs written down.

LBCo carries demolition through FF&E placement, including receipt and installation of all owner-supplied millwork — roughly 30 furniture pieces plus 22 built-ins including 18 Suitwave units. Millwork supply, permit fees, hazmat, low-voltage and sign fabrication are outside the number.

Included — 13

  1. Demolition and removal.

    Selective demolition per HBC D-101: partitions, platforms, the full acoustical ceiling system, the existing door with frame remaining, alcove trim, and selective drywall for FRT blocking access. Club Monaco logo and signage removal per owner direction, dumpsters and hauling included.

  2. Drywall and framing.

    New partitions, headers, ceilings, soffits and column wraps per the HBC set, plus platform and curb framing, FRT plywood decking and FRT in-wall blocking at new assemblies. A $3,000 allowance carries Level-5 finish at the fitting room partitions receiving wallcovering.

  3. Carpentry and trim.

    HM door, frame and hardware supply and install; WM-01 base and WM-02 cap supply and install with profile match per open RFI. Trim at modified cabinets and hanging accessories are included.

  4. GC-built items and blocking.

    Blocking and reinforcement at existing walls, the SWB shopwindow block, display-window plinths with heights set on site, and the MRC.01 mirror-cladding substrate. All four are marked “Made by GC” on the Sourcing Overview.

  5. Millwork installation.

    Receipt and installation of all owner-supplied millwork: ~30 furniture pieces plus 22 built-ins including 18 Suitwave units and new components at reused cabinets. Installation of display-window lightbox 501.1 produced by TeamWorks is carried here.

  6. Mirrors and glazing.

    Priced to a received glazing quotation verified against LBCo’s ~767 SF field takeoff, including 16 LED mirror units, MRC.01 cladding, cabinet back panels and podium facets. NJ capital-improvement tax treatment is applied to the material portion.

  7. Painting and wall covering.

    The BM OC-152 paint system with Room B blue by approved local custom match. Wallcovering installation only across approximately 4,985 SF with paper supplied by Suit Supply, plus exterior storefront repaint in black including the wood door.

  8. Flooring.

    Light board patching, prep, and full sand to bare wood plus Saicos Ecoline pitch-black application over approximately 3,900 SF. Product is supplied by Suit Supply and applied by LBCo.

  9. Electrical.

    Millwork and mirror LED feeds, circuit reuse at removed fixtures, camera-removal make-safe, and installation of added track lighting with heads supplied by owner. The electrician files his own NJ permit under LBCo’s master.

  10. Fire protection.

    Existing heads receive concealed color-match replacement only — no additions, no relocations. Shop drawings and permit are included; the mall sprinkler shutoff fee is carried at cost as a marked allowance.

  11. Signage and storefront.

    Receipt, inspection and installation of owner-supplied signage per landlord criteria, opaque vinyl application at storefront glazing, and an allowance for sign engineering, procurement and permit.

  12. FF&E logistics.

    Receiving, staging and placement of sourced items from Miami (M1–M10), Short Hills (SH1–SH2) and the US Warehouse, mannequins included.

  13. General conditions, supervision and PM.

    Superintendent and project manager for nine weeks, plus site labor, temporary protection, temporary power coordination, equipment, documentation and final cleans.

Excluded — 12

  1. Millwork supply.

    Installation only; the millworker provides units and integral LEDs. Suit Supply direction of July 6 and July 9.

  2. Permit and municipal fees.

    All permit and municipal fees, landlord fees, deposits and utility connection costs sit with the owner. Trades file under LBCo’s master permit.

  3. Concrete work.

    All concrete is out of trade pricing; a $1,800 marked concrete allowance covers the platform curb cut only, extent per field conditions.

  4. Hazmat and structural demolition.

    Abatement, concrete and subfloor demolition, structural and rooftop removals and thin-set or mastic removal are all outside the demolition scope.

  5. Low-voltage, data, security, AV.

    Outside the electrical basis. The sound-system rack is handled by the Suit Supply AV vendor per the owner RFI response of July 27.

  6. Sign fabrication and window vinyl material.

    Puur+ sheet 300 directs verification with Brandsigns and sizing with marketing. Order of magnitude $14,000 if fabrication is directed to LBCo.

  7. New acoustical ceiling.

    Grid and tile supply and reinstallation carried at $0 pending confirmation that no new ACT is required.

  8. Inbound freight, owner-sourced items.

    Arranged and paid by Suit Supply to the dock. LBCo prices receiving, staging and placement only.

  9. Fire alarm modifications.

    No fire alarm modifications are included, per the fire protection basis of the HBC set.

  10. Work at the existing exit door.

    Confirmed by owner RFI response of July 27 that there is no work at the existing exit door.

  11. Owner-supplied materials.

    Wallpapers, Saicos Ecoline product, specialty textiles, rugs, acrylics AC.x and Hay racks are free-issued to site; LBCo installs and applies only.

  12. Contingent added scope.

    Three items are handled by change proposal if field conditions require, listed below.

03Method

The July 22 register ranked fifteen cost-impact questions instead of pricing guesses.

Five high-impact items, six medium, four low — issued in writing so Hélène Tran could close the dollars that actually move the number. Every unresolved line is a marked allowance trued by change proposal per Clarification 16.

01

Ask the questions instead of padding the lines.

LBCo issued a ranked cost-impact RFI register on July 22 rather than burying unknowns in wide allowances. Five items were flagged high impact, six medium and four low, ordered by dollar exposure.

02

Every unresolved item is a marked allowance.

Ten lines are identified as allowances in Clarification 16 and trued to final subcontractor pricing by change proposal. The allowance log is maintained by the project coordinator against the RFI register.

03

Landlord fees pass through at cost.

The $1,500 mall sprinkler shutdown and recharge fee is paid by LBCo direct to the mall with no markup applied. It is trued to the mall’s actual invoice at closeout.

04

Nine-week union build to a fixed opening.

Construction runs August 17 to the October 16 opening with superintendent and project manager carried for the full nine weeks. Millwork deliveries are assumed from ~September 21 and phased deliveries are acceptable.

05

Joint field verification before glass is cut.

Mirror and glazing sizes are per LBCo takeoff with final dimensions confirmed by joint field verification. Plinth and mirror heights are set on site against the installed substrate.

06

NJ tax handled as a capital improvement.

A 6.625% provision is carried on estimated material portions of trades not pricing tax in their own scope. Labor is untaxed under the capital improvement rule with ST-8 executed by Suit Supply at award.

07

Trades permit under LBCo’s master.

The electrician and the fire protection subcontractor file their own NJ permits under LBCo’s master permit. Township approvals therefore move in parallel with the compressed schedule.

08

Temporary protection and power carried by LBCo.

Site protection is LBCo’s, and subcontractor-required 24/7 temporary electric is assumed available from the existing house service at no added cost. Any metered temporary service required by the mall would be an added cost.

Pricing Mechanics

Tax. New Jersey treats this work as a capital improvement. A 6.625% provision of $3,137.60 is carried on estimated material portions of trades that did not price tax in their scope, labor is untaxed, and Certificate of Capital Improvement ST-8 is executed by Suit Supply at award.

Permitting. The electrical and fire protection subcontractors file their own New Jersey permits under LBCo’s master permit so township approvals run parallel to demolition and framing rather than behind them.

Field verification. Mirror and glazing sizes are confirmed jointly in the field before any glass is cut, and plinth and mirror heights are set on site against installed substrate rather than scaled from the design set.

Site services. Temporary protection is carried by LBCo for the full nine weeks. Subcontractor-required 24/7 temporary electric is assumed available from the existing house service at no added cost.

The black storefront repaint contradicts Puur+ sheet 300. Plan 100 and the Sourcing Overview disagree on LED mirror count. Saicos Ecoline cannot go over an existing finish.

Conflict 01

Storefront repaint.

Document A

Owner added a full black exterior repaint including the wood door on July 28.

Document B

Puur+ sheet 300 directs KEEP EXISTING MARBLE and KEEP EXISTING BRASS LINE.

LBCo Position

No dimensioned storefront elevation exists. LBCo scaled roughly 34'-7" of frontage at about 10'-2" floor to head and carried a $12,000 marked allowance pending a painter site walk.

Conflict 02

LED mirror count.

Document A

Plan 100 shows 16 LED mirror glass units.

Document B

The Sourcing Overview shows 14 units.

LBCo Position

Glazing is priced to a received quotation verified against LBCo’s ~767 SF field takeoff. Count and sizes are confirmed by joint field verification before any glass is cut.

Conflict 03

Floor finish.

Document A

Saicos Ecoline penetrating oil requires bare wood to take.

Document B

An existing finish is in place across approximately 3,900 SF of floor.

LBCo Position

A full sand is a product requirement, not an upgrade. The allowance moved from $14,500 to $30,000 and the owner confirms Ecoline oil versus a literal black floor paint.

Conflict 04

Governing set split.

Document A

HBC Bid Set 07/01/2026 governs construction, including D-101.

Document B

Puur+ Complete Set 12-06 supplies design intent, finishes and millwork.

LBCo Position

Each set governs its own content. Conflicts between the two are flagged as open RFIs and priced as marked allowances rather than absorbed into the lump sum.

Conflict 05

Acoustical ceiling.

Document A

D-101 demolishes the existing acoustical ceiling system in full.

Document B

No new ACT is shown anywhere in the HBC or Puur+ sets.

LBCo Position

Grid and tile supply and reinstallation are carried at $0 pending owner confirmation. BOH ceilings are assumed drywall soffit or exposed per the HBC reflected ceiling plan.

09Commitments

Allowances are marked, and conflicts are flagged rather than built.

Two positions LBCo holds through buyout on the Short Hills pop-up. Both were established in writing before award.

01

Allowances are marked, not buried.

Ten specific lines are identified as allowances in Clarification 16 and trued to final subcontractor pricing by change proposal at buyout: storefront repaint, flooring prep and application, the concrete curb cut, Level-5 drywall at the fitting rooms, painting and wallcovering, dumpster volume beyond demolition, molding and door supply, display-window plinths, sign permit and engineering, and the mall sprinkler shutoff fee.

The $1,500 mall sprinkler shutoff and recharge fee is paid by LBCo direct to the mall at cost with no markup applied and trued to the mall’s actual invoice. The NJ sales tax provision of $3,137.60 is trued to actual once material portions are known at buyout.

02

We flag conflicts in the documents rather than build them.

LBCo identified that a full black storefront repaint conflicts with Puur+ sheet 300’s KEEP EXISTING MARBLE and KEEP EXISTING BRASS LINE notes, that Plan 100 and the Sourcing Overview disagree on LED mirror count at 16 versus 14 units, and that Saicos Ecoline requires bare wood and therefore a full sand across approximately 3,900 SF.

Each of the three was raised in writing before award rather than discovered later as a change order. The July 22 register carries them with the eleven other cost-impact questions, ranked so the awarding authority can close the dollars in order of exposure.
