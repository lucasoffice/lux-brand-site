03
Approach and Sequencing

Four sequencing questions Springfield has to answer before the schedule locks.

Phasing, landlord-permitted work hours, long-lead procurement, and mockup review drive the Springfield sequence. Long-lead tracking and the brand mockup session are already fixed as method; the first two items depend on the work letter.

Two of the four items below are questions. Two are methods LUX runs on every boutique buildout regardless of what the work letter says. The distinction matters for pricing. Methods sit inside general conditions once general conditions have a duration. Questions cannot be priced at all until they are answered.

Sequencing for a boutique of this type follows a fixed order. Permit issues, barricade and protection go up, demolition runs, then the space is surveyed a second time against as-found conditions. Rough-in follows in trade order — overhead mechanical, then electrical and low-voltage, then framing closeout, then ceiling. Finishes run floor-up. Millwork sets last, into a clean, aimed, commissioned shell.

What changes that order is occupancy. A dark space compresses the schedule and lets demolition and rough-in overlap across the full floor plate. A phased occupancy splits the plate, doubles the barricade, doubles the mobilizations, and pushes trades into after-hours windows the landlord may not grant. The delta between those two conditions is significant and it is not estimated here.

Long-lead procurement is the second schedule driver. Millwork, lighting, and specialty glass are the three categories that historically set the turnover date on a boutique of this size. They are logged weekly from award, not from submittal approval.
